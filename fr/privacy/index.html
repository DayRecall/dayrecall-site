Key points

  • DayRecall works on your devices. We have no user accounts and no servers that receive your audio, transcripts, voiceprint, places or health data, and we cannot access them.
  • Speech is turned into text on your iPhone. The audio is cleared automatically once it has been transcribed: DayRecall keeps the text, not the audio.
  • Your AI engine is this iPhone by default. If you choose and allow your own API, new transcripts can be sent automatically to find to-dos, and a day’s conversations are sent when you request a recap. Questions and matching excerpts are also sent when you ask. You can separately share a day with an AI app. Other information may leave through Apple services you use, such as iCloud Backup; Section 6 explains these cases.
  • No advertising, no analytics, no tracking. We do not sell personal information.

These key points are a summary. The full policy below governs.

1. About this policy

1.1 This Privacy Policy explains how personal information is handled in connection with the DayRecall app for iPhone and Apple Watch (the “App”) and the website at dayrecall.com (the “Website”; together with the App, the “Services”).

1.2 The App is designed to process your information on your own devices. Most of the information described in this policy is never sent to us, and we cannot see it. We describe it so that you know what the App does and what you control. Section 7 sets out the limited information that does reach us.

1.3 This policy should be read together with our Terms of Service.

2. Who is responsible

2.1 The Services are provided by DayRecall (“DayRecall”, “we”, “us” or “our”). For the information described in Section 7, DayRecall is the controller (or personal information handler): we decide why and how that information is processed. You can contact us at support@dayrecall.com.

2.2 Information that the App processes only on your devices, described in Sections 4 and 5, is not collected by us. It remains under your control on your devices, and we have no access to it.

3. Definitions

In this policy:

  • “personal information” means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person, and includes “personal data” as defined by applicable law;
  • “your devices” means the iPhone and Apple Watch on which you use the App;
  • “listening hours” means the times you choose for the App to listen;
  • “transcript” means the text the App produces from speech;
  • “voiceprint” means a set of numbers derived from your voice that the App uses to recognise you as a speaker; it is not an audio recording;
  • “health data” means the sleep, steps, walking distance, workouts, heart rate and resting heart rate that the App reads from the Health app on your iPhone, if you turn that on (Section 4.9);
  • “third-party service” means a product or service that we do not operate, such as Apple’s services, a cloud service you connect, or another app you share content with.

4. Information processed on your devices

The App processes the following information on your devices, only to provide the features you use. Unless Section 6 says otherwise, it stays on your devices.

4.1 Audio and speech

(a) During your listening hours, the App uses the microphone of your Apple Watch or iPhone. An on-device speech-detection model keeps only the parts in which someone is speaking; other sound is discarded. Speech segments are held temporarily in compressed form and passed from Apple Watch to iPhone over Apple’s connection between the two devices, about every two hours and when listening stops. Each segment is deleted from Apple Watch once your iPhone confirms that it has arrived.

(b) On your iPhone, each segment is transcribed on the device using Apple’s speech recognition. The audio is deleted automatically once its transcript has been saved. If transcription of a segment fails, its audio is kept on your iPhone so that transcription can be retried, until it succeeds or you delete the recording. The same applies when you record with your iPhone and to audio files you import; for an imported file, the App works on its own copy.

(c) If you turn on Stand by to take over, your iPhone’s microphone stays active during your listening hours so that your iPhone can take over at once if your Apple Watch stops. Nothing is kept while it is standing by.

4.2 Transcripts and information derived from them

The App stores on your iPhone:

  1. transcripts, with the time each part was said and the device that heard it;
  2. the to-dos, deadlines and meetings the App identifies in them, what you did with each, and saved recaps and intermediate recap notes;
  3. a search index of your transcripts; and
  4. your settings, such as your listening hours.

4.3 AI engine, questions and recaps

In Settings › AI engine, you choose the engine that identifies to-dos, deadlines and meetings, writes recaps and answers questions. The default is On this iPhone, using Apple’s on-device model, part of Apple Intelligence, where available. You may instead choose My own API; the information sent to that service is described in Section 6.2. The App does not switch engines on its own. Ordinary questions and answers are not kept after the App stops running. Recaps written in the App, intermediate notes used to write them, and AI-app answers you bring back and keep with a day are saved locally in your library. A provider you choose may retain what it receives under its own terms.

4.4 Voiceprint (optional)

(a) If you turn on Mark who is speaking, the App asks you to read five short cards aloud, about a minute in total, and creates your voiceprint from that recording on your iPhone. The recording is deleted as soon as the voiceprint has been created. The App keeps your voiceprint on that iPhone and does not automatically send it to us or anyone else. It is excluded from system backups and iCloud. A password-encrypted manual backup that you create in Developer options can include it, as described in Section 6.

(b) To tell speakers apart, the App compares the voices in a conversation with your voiceprint while it processes that conversation. It keeps no voiceprint of anyone else. Other speakers are labelled Other 1, Other 2 and so on, and the numbering starts again in each conversation.

(c) A voiceprint is biometric information, which many laws treat as sensitive. The App creates one only after you turn the feature on and complete the recording. You can delete it at any time with Delete my voice. If you do not use this feature, the rest of the App works normally; it simply does not mark who is speaking.

4.5 Places (optional)

Places are off until you turn on Show the day on a map in Settings › Places and allow location access when iOS asks. If you allow access Always, places can be noted while the App is closed. The App records the places where you stay (times of arrival and departure, coordinates and a place name) and keeps them on your iPhone. To name places and draw maps, the App uses Apple Maps (Section 6.1). If you include conversations with recorded places in a recap written by your own API, or in a day shared with another app, those places are included too (Sections 6.2 and 6.3).

4.6 Calendar and Reminders (optional)

(a) Nothing is added to your calendar or Reminders until you confirm it. An event you confirm opens in the iOS New Event screen. Events from your weekly plan are added once you allow the App to add events.

(b) If you turn on Add confirmed to-dos to Reminders (off by default), to-dos you confirm are added to Reminders and are marked complete there when you complete them in the App.

(c) If you turn on Name the timeline after meetings (off by default), the App reads calendar event titles, account names, start and end times and all-day flags on your iPhone to label parts of your day. The inputs used by the App are retained in a protected local context cache, excluded from system backups. They are not sent to us, but can be included when you choose a password-encrypted manual backup (Section 6.1). Restoring that context does not write to your system calendars; calendar changes during replay stay in a separate local layer.

4.7 Notifications

Notifications, such as the evening list of items to confirm and reminders of due to-dos, are scheduled on your iPhone and Apple Watch, with no server of ours involved. You can turn each kind on or off in Settings › Notifications.

4.8 Diagnostic logs

To help fix problems, the App keeps a log on your iPhone and Apple Watch of what it did, such as counts, durations, settings and errors. The log does not contain what was said, your questions or answers, names, places or calendar titles. When the App crashes or stops responding, iOS reports this to the App afterwards, and the report is added to the same log. Logs are kept for up to 14 days and are excluded from system backups. They leave your iPhone only when you choose to share a log or a password-encrypted manual backup from Developer options.

4.9 Health data (optional)

(a) Reading health data is off until you turn on Show sleep, activity and heart rate in Settings › Health and allow access when iOS asks. Only then does the App read the following from the Health app on your iPhone, through Apple’s HealthKit, whichever device or app recorded it: sleep, steps and walking distance, workouts, heart rate and resting heart rate. The App reads only the kinds you allow. It only reads from Health and never writes to it.

(b) The App uses health data to show on your day’s timeline whether you were asleep, walking or working out during a part of the day, or had a heart rate faster than usual, with the numbers behind it on that part’s detail page. When you ask what was said during an activity, such as “What did I say while I was running?” or “What did we talk about before bed?”, it uses the data to find the times you mean. It also retains the inputs used by those features for the local backup and replay described below.

(c) The App saves the health inputs it reads and uses in a protected local context cache, excluded from system backups. This includes sleep intervals, workouts and recorded distances, five-minute step and walking-distance buckets, heart readings and the resting baseline used for each day. A password-encrypted manual backup can include this cache, calendar context, the captured ranges and status, the source time zone and a reference clock. This is a replay of inputs available to the App within its history and cached coverage, not an export of the entire Apple Health database or unauthorized kinds. HealthKit does not reveal whether an empty result means access was denied or no data exists; the App records that visibility limit. A query or persistence failure stops backup creation instead of silently producing incomplete context. During replay, the App reads only the saved context; missing, damaged or out-of-range context does not fall back to the test iPhone’s Health or calendars. Nothing is written back to Health. These inputs are not sent to us. The diagnostic log (Section 4.8) records counts, never the health values.

(d) Raw Health records and numerical HealthKit measurements are not automatically attached to requests that find to-dos or answer questions through your own API. On the recap and hand-over page, Add health data is off by default. Turning it on asks for permission; your choice is remembered and can be turned off there. An earlier choice to include labels does not turn on this numerical-data option. When it is on, the selected day includes available activity labels and values: heart rate averages and reading counts near conversations; the day’s heart rate average and range, resting baseline, steps and walking or running distance; and sleep, workouts and walks with their times, durations and recorded distances. Missing values are left out. If you tap Write it here with My own API selected, that service receives the included labels and values; if you hand the day to another app, that app receives them. The recipient handles the content under its own terms. The Shortcuts action Hand today to an AI never includes Health information. For questions about what was said during an activity, the App uses Health locally to choose the times to search without attaching the underlying Health records to the question or excerpts.

(e) We do not use health data for advertising, marketing or data mining. The App draws no medical conclusions and gives no health advice: it states only what the data shows, for example that your heart rate was faster than usual.

(f) Health data is health information, which many laws treat as sensitive. New Health reads require the feature to be on and access to be allowed. You can turn it off in Settings › Health, or withdraw access in the iPhone Settings app (Health › Data Access & Devices). Turning it off stops new reads and marks any earlier cache as retained; it does not delete that cache or exported files. Delete all memories also removes saved health and calendar context. In replay, an explicitly restored snapshot can still supply its recorded context without accessing this iPhone’s Health. The rest of the App works without live Health access.

6. When information leaves your devices

Information processed by the App leaves your devices only in the cases below. In each case it goes to a third-party service, not to us, unless Section 7 says otherwise.

6.1 Apple services

  1. Backups. If you use iCloud Backup or back up your iPhone to a computer, iOS includes the App’s transcripts, to-dos, places and any audio still on your iPhone. Your voiceprint, logs, recorded health and calendar context, and developer replay observations are excluded from system backups. Backups are made and stored by Apple or on your computer, under their terms; we have no access to them.
  2. Encrypted manual backups. Developer options lets you create and share a password-encrypted copy of local App data and settings, including the voiceprint, testing records, logs, your custom AI API key and the saved health and calendar inputs described in Section 4.9. Captured ranges, status, time zone and a reference clock let the App replay those inputs without using another iPhone’s live context. The password is not saved in the backup or sent to us. You choose where the file is stored or shared. Restoration replaces local App state, keeps a private recovery snapshot and preserves replay observations, including both versions of conflicting records. It does not import data into system Health or Calendar. System permissions, external accounts and their full Health, Calendar or Reminders databases, and Watch data not yet received by this iPhone are not included.
  3. Apple Maps. If you use Places, Apple receives the locations the App looks up and displays, under Apple’s Privacy Policy.
  4. System features. Speech recognition, Apple Intelligence and translation run on your iPhone. iOS may download the language resources they need from Apple.
  5. Calendar and Reminders. Events and reminders you add are kept and synced by Calendar and Reminders with the accounts you have set up on your iPhone, such as iCloud, Google or Outlook.
  6. App analytics. If you have turned on Share With App Developers in the iPhone Settings app (Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements), Apple shares crash reports and usage statistics about the App with us (Section 7).

6.2 Your own API (optional)

(a) In Settings › AI engine, you can choose My own API, an OpenAI-compatible service used with your own key, instead of the model on this iPhone. Before personal content is sent, the App explains the types of information it will send to that service and asks for permission. A different service domain or an expanded permission scope requires permission again. Permission given under the earlier questions-and-excerpts notice does not authorise the expanded uses below.

(b) Once you select this engine and give permission, the App sends the service:

  • To find to-dos: newly transcribed text, with available recording dates, times, time zones and speaker labels, and relevant existing to-do information used for comparison, automatically in batches, including during background processing. These requests can occur without you asking a question.
  • To write a recap: when you tap Write it here, the selected day’s conversations that you have left included, their times and speaker labels, recorded places if available, and your selected instructions. Health information is included only as described in Section 4.9(d).
  • To answer a question: your question and, for a question about your recordings, selected matching transcript excerpts with available recording dates, times, time zones, speaker labels and recording devices, together with the current date, time and time zone. To help search for those excerpts, the App may first send the question on its own to request related search terms. That first request can occur even if no matching excerpts are later found and no answer request follows.

(c) Audio and your voiceprint are never sent to the API. Recorded places and optional health information may be included in a recap as described above; Health records are not automatically attached to to-do or question requests. Test connection sends a fixed test message without your questions or transcripts. Loading the service’s model list sends no questions or transcripts. Your key is stored in the iOS Keychain on this iPhone and used to authenticate requests to your service. For activity questions, the prompt may state that excerpts were selected from recorded activity intervals; the underlying Health records are not sent. The library database and backup archives are not uploaded to the API.

(d) The service processes what it receives under its own terms and privacy policy and may store it. Choose a service you trust with the included conversations, including what other people said. You can withdraw permission with Stop sharing with … in the same settings. This selects On this iPhone for to-dos, recaps and answers; the on-device model must be available for those features to work. Withdrawing permission cannot recall information already sent to the provider.

6.3 Handing a day to another app

(a) Hand the day to an AI app creates a text file of the selected conversations from a day: what was said and when, speaker labels if enabled, recorded places if available, optional activity labels and health values if you choose to add them (Section 4.9(d)), and your selected instructions. You decide where the file goes through the iOS share sheet, or you can copy the text. This sharing route does not send the text to your API by itself; using Write it here with My own API selected is the separate route described in Section 6.2. The other app handles the content it receives under its own terms.

(b) In the Shortcuts app, Hand today to an AI copies today’s conversations to your iPhone’s clipboard only, not to your other devices, and the copy is cleared after one hour. Bring the AI’s answer back reads the clipboard only when you run it and allow pasting, finds to-dos for you to confirm, and can save the returned answer with its day as a recap.

6.4 Logs you share

A log you share goes where you send it. If you send it to us, Section 7 applies.

6.5 Optional model directory updates

(a) The App includes an offline service and model directory. Opening it does not contact the directory source. Each time you choose Update model directory, the App explains the connection and asks whether you agree to that one download from models.dev. Cancelling leaves your saved directory available. The App does not remember permission for future downloads and never refreshes this directory automatically.

(b) The download is a request for public directory information. It does not send your API key, recordings, transcripts, questions, health information or a device identifier. models.dev and its hosting providers receive your IP address, request time and basic connection information, and may retain access logs under their own rules. This request goes directly to the directory source, not to us. We do not receive its access logs.

(c) A successfully downloaded and checked directory is cached on your iPhone for offline use and excluded from system backups. A later successful download replaces it; removing the App removes its local cache. Downloading does not change your selected AI service, model, API key or content-sharing permission. Choosing and confirming a different service address is a separate action: the App removes the previous address's saved key and permission, and you must provide the new service's key and allow content sharing before using it.

7. Information we receive

We receive personal information only in the following cases:

  1. When you contact us. If you write to us, we receive your email address, your name if you include it, the content of your message and any attachments, such as a log or screenshots.
  2. Beta testing. If you test the App through Apple’s TestFlight, Apple shares with us crash logs and usage information, such as installs, sessions and crashes, your device model and system version. If you were invited by email, we also see your name and email address. Feedback and screenshots you send through TestFlight come to us. Apple describes this in TestFlight & Privacy.
  3. Apple analytics. If you have turned on Share With App Developers, Apple shares crash reports and usage statistics about the App with us. App Store Connect also gives us aggregated statistics, such as numbers of downloads, that do not identify you.
  4. Purchases. Any purchase made through the App Store is processed by Apple. We do not receive your payment card details.

We do not otherwise collect personal information through the App. You are not required to provide personal information to us: if you do not contact us or take part in beta testing, we receive none.

The Website. dayrecall.com uses no cookies, no analytics and no third-party scripts or fonts, and we receive no information about its visitors. The Website is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub logs visitors’ IP addresses for security purposes, as described in GitHub’s documentation, and processes them under the GitHub Privacy Statement.

10. International transfers

We and our service providers may process the information described in Section 7 in countries other than the one where you live, including the United States, whose data protection laws may differ from yours. Where the law requires, we put appropriate safeguards in place for such transfers, such as standard contractual clauses approved by the European Commission or the United Kingdom. Section 14.3 applies to personal information from mainland China. Information that the App processes only on your devices is not transferred by us.

11. Retention

  1. Audio is deleted automatically once it has been transcribed. If transcription fails, it is kept until transcription succeeds or you delete the recording. Speech segments on Apple Watch are deleted once your iPhone confirms that they have arrived.
  2. Transcripts, to-dos, places and settings stay on your iPhone until you delete them or the App. Copies in backups remain until those backups are replaced or deleted.
  3. Your voiceprint stays on your iPhone until you delete it or the App.
  4. Ordinary questions and answers are not kept once the App stops running. Saved recaps, including answers brought back from an AI app, remain in your library until you delete them or the App. Intermediate recap notes are stored locally for reuse; older notes may be cleared automatically. Delete all memories clears saved recaps and their notes. Copies in backups remain until those backups are replaced or deleted.
  5. Saved health and calendar context remains in the protected local cache until you delete all memories, replace the local context through restoration or remove the App. Turning a source off stops new reads but retains earlier cached inputs. Copies in exported backups remain until you delete those files separately.
  6. Logs are kept on your devices for up to 14 days.
  7. The copy made by Hand today to an AI is cleared from the clipboard after one hour.
  8. Information we receive is kept only as long as necessary for the purpose for which we received it, such as resolving your request, and is then deleted, unless the law requires us to keep it longer. You can ask us to delete it sooner. TestFlight information and analytics are retained by Apple under its own policies.

12. Security

12.1 The App protects its data on your iPhone with iOS Data Protection, so its files cannot be read until your iPhone has been unlocked after it starts, and it stores your API key in the iOS Keychain. Because the App does not send your content to us, that content cannot be exposed through our systems.

12.2 We protect the information we receive with reasonable technical and organisational measures. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure, so please protect your devices with a passcode and keep their software up to date. If a security incident affects personal information we hold, we will notify you and the relevant authorities as the law requires.

13. Your rights and choices

13.1 Controls in the App

  1. Delete a recording, with its transcript and to-dos: Delete recording on the recording’s page. Delete a saved recap with Delete this recap on its own page.
  2. Delete memories, including recordings, transcripts, to-dos, recaps and their notes, places, the search index and saved health and calendar context: Settings › Privacy › Delete all memories. Separate developer backup files and test records are not deleted by this control.
  3. Delete your voiceprint: Delete my voice.
  4. Get a copy of a day: Hand the day to an AI app creates a text file, which you can also save from the share sheet.
  5. Stop places, meeting names or Reminders: turn the feature off in Settings, or withdraw the permission in the iPhone Settings app.
  6. Stop reading health data: turn the feature off in Settings › Health, or withdraw the permission in the iPhone Settings app (Health › Data Access & Devices).
  7. Stop using your own cloud API: Stop sharing with …; remove your key with Remove saved key.
  8. Delete the App from your iPhone and Apple Watch: this removes what it stored on them. Copies in earlier backups remain until those backups are replaced or deleted. iOS may keep a saved API key in the Keychain after the App is deleted, so remove the key first.

13.2 Your legal rights

(a) Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access the personal information we hold about you and receive a copy of it, to have it corrected or deleted, to restrict or object to its processing, to receive it in a portable format, to withdraw your consent, and to ask us to explain how we process it. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection authority.

(b) Because most information stays on your devices, you can exercise these rights over it directly in the App, as described in Section 13.1. We cannot access, retrieve or delete information on your devices or in your backups.

(c) For information we hold, write to support@dayrecall.com. We may need to verify your identity before acting on a request. We will respond within the time required by applicable law, and we will not discriminate against you for exercising your rights.

14. Information for certain regions

14.1 European Economic Area, United Kingdom and Switzerland

Our legal bases for processing the information we receive are set out in Section 8. You may lodge a complaint with the data protection authority where you live or work, or where you believe an infringement occurred. Your voiceprint (Section 4.4) and, if you turn it on, your health data (Section 4.9) are processed only on your device, at your explicit request, and are never received by us.

14.2 United States

In the past 12 months we have received only the categories of personal information described in Section 7: identifiers, such as your name and email address if you contact us or are invited to TestFlight; the content of your messages; and device and diagnostic information, such as crash logs. We received them from you and from Apple, and use them for the purposes in Section 8. We do not sell or share personal information, as those terms are defined in California law, and we do not use or disclose sensitive personal information for purposes that would give you a right to limit it. Depending on your state, you may ask to know, access, correct or delete the personal information we hold about you, yourself or through an authorised agent, by writing to support@dayrecall.com.

14.3 Mainland China

(a) DayRecall is the personal information handler for the information described in Section 7, and can be contacted at support@dayrecall.com.

(b) The App processes the following sensitive personal information only on your device and only with your separate consent, which you give when you turn the feature on: your voiceprint (biometric information), used to mark which lines you said; the places where you stay (location tracks), used to show your day on a map; and your health data (health information), used to show what you were doing during your day. They are necessary only for those features, and if you do not turn the features on, the rest of the App works normally. Because they are never sent to us, we do not provide them to anyone or transfer them outside mainland China. The exception is the information you explicitly choose to include in a recap written by your own API or a day handed to another app (Sections 4.9(d), 6.2 and 6.3), which goes to your chosen recipient.

(c) If you contact us or take part in beta testing, the information described in Section 7 is processed by our email provider and by Apple, which may store it outside mainland China, for the purposes in Section 8. By choosing to send it to us, you separately consent to that transfer. You can exercise your rights over it by writing to us.

15. Children

The Services are not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. You must be at least 13 to use the App. If you are under the age at which you can consent to the processing of your personal information where you live, for example under 14 in mainland China or under 16 in some countries of the European Union, you may use the App only with the consent of your parent or guardian. If you believe a child has sent us personal information without the required consent, contact us and we will delete it.

16. Recording other people

16.1 The App captures what is said by the people around you. Their speech becomes transcripts on your devices, and we do not receive it.

16.2 Laws on recording conversations and on privacy differ between places, and some require the consent of everyone taking part, even when only text is kept. You are responsible for telling the people you talk with that DayRecall is listening, for obtaining any consent the law requires, and for how you use and share their words.
